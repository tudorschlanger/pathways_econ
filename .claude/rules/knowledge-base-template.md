# Course Knowledge Base: Pathways — Economics & Finance Workshop

## Notation Registry

| Rule | Convention | Example | Anti-Pattern |
|------|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Opportunity cost | Value of next-best alternative only, not all foregone options | "The OC of the run is sleeping in (her 2nd choice)" | "The OC is studying + working + sleeping" |
| Sunk cost | Past costs that cannot be recovered; irrelevant to forward-looking decisions | "The ticket money is gone no matter what" | "I already paid $50, I should stay" |
| Cost-benefit analysis | Explicitly list costs and benefits in separate columns before deciding | Two-column table with dollar values | Gut-feel decision without enumeration |
| Marginal thinking | Compare additional cost vs. additional benefit of one more unit | "One more hour studying costs $12 in foregone wages, benefits 5 points" | Total cost vs. total benefit |
| Causal reasoning | Cause must precede effect; control for confounders; use DAGs | X -> Y with Z blocked | Correlation implies causation |
| Supply/Demand | Price on vertical axis, quantity on horizontal | Standard S-D graph | Inverted axes |
| Equilibrium | Where supply meets demand; no surplus or shortage | Intersection point | Any other point called "equilibrium" |
| Externalities | Costs or benefits borne by third parties not part of the transaction | Pollution from factory affects neighbors | Only counting private costs/benefits |
| Prospect theory | Losses loom larger than gains; reference dependence; diminishing sensitivity | -$100 hurts more than +$100 pleases | Assuming symmetric utility function |

## Design Principles

| Principle | Evidence | Lectures Applied |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| Concrete before abstract | Cognitive load theory | All (start with examples before definitions) |
| Active learning | Higher retention vs. passive lecture | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (group activities, games, debates) |
| Progressive complexity | Build on prior lectures | All (sequential, each lecture assumes previous) |
| Socratic questioning | Deeper engagement than monologue | Embedded questions in slides |
| Real-world relevance | Student motivation and retention | All (policy debates, games, relatable scenarios) |
| No more than 3 key ideas per lecture | Memory limits for high school audience | All |

## Anti-Patterns (Don't Do This)

| Anti-Pattern | What Happened | Correction |
|-------------|---------------|-----------|
| Bundled activities | Students confused by 4-5 activities at once | One concept per activity, build sequentially |
| Political or stereotypical examples | Alienates some students | Use neutral, universally relatable scenarios |
| Too much content per lecture | Students remember nothing | Strip down to 2-3 takeaways per lecture |
| Disconnected theories | Prospect theory unrelated to market puzzles | Explicitly link theory to observed behavior |
